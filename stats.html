SCENES Overview

Last modified:

August 18, 2025

This page gives a quick look at what’s inside the dataset. It shows how the images are distributed by view type, time, scene, weather, and lighting. Each section highlights the main patterns you can spot from the plots.

View

Outdoor shots make up the largest part of the dataset. Indoor scenes are split between spaces with windows (plenty of natural light) and enclosed rooms without windows. This balance makes it possible to compare natural vs. artificial lighting conditions.

Temporal

Data collection happened in bursts over several months, reflecting field campaigns and site availability. Outdoor shots are more evenly spread, while indoor sessions are clustered into shorter time windows.

Most captures were taken between 9 AM and 5 PM, peaking around midday. That matches typical daylight hours but still leaves good coverage in the morning and late afternoon.

Scene

The dataset covers a mix of environments. On the outdoor side, countryside and forest dominate, while for indoor shots, offices lead the way, with some extra coverage from conference rooms, corridors, and bedrooms.

Capture times follow expectations: outdoor scenes center on the middle of the day, while indoor environments stretch later into the afternoon.

Weather & Lighting

Most images come from clear or partly cloudy days. There’s also a fair amount of overcast/cloudy coverage, which helps include softer light. Rare conditions like rain, fog, drizzle, and snow are present but limited.

Lighting conditions are mostly daylight, with a smaller but useful set of mixed light (daylight + electric) and a handful of purely artificial light samples. This gives the dataset range across natural and human-made lighting setups.

Overall, the dataset offers a broad mix of outdoor and indoor scenes, captured across different times of day, seasons, weather conditions, and lighting setups. Outdoor shots dominate, but there’s still solid coverage of indoor environments, including both naturally lit and windowless spaces.

This diversity is intentional: it makes the dataset useful for studying how context (scene, weather, or lighting) affects image appearance and for training models that need to generalize beyond a single environment. In other words, the numbers and plots above aren’t just statistics — they set the stage for building models that are robust to real-world variability.
